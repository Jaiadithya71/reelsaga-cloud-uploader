# ReelSaga: YouTube vs Instagram

Analytics snapshot | October 4, 2026 | @ReelSagaShorts and @reelsaga3000

Bottom line: Instagram is reaching far more people outside the follower base. YouTube has little Shorts-feed distribution in this snapshot. This identifies a distribution gap, not its cause, and does not prove that content quality is or is not a problem.

## 1. The numbers

YouTube's last-seven-days report covers September 26-October 2. Instagram shows "Last 7 days" without exact date bounds in the web dashboard. Treat this as a directional comparison, not a strictly matched experiment.

| Metric | YouTube | Instagram |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Views | 282 | 13,060 |
| Additional view metric | 128 engaged views | 5,857 viewers |
| Audience growth | 0 new subscribers | 12 followers reported |
| Top item in seven-day report | 14 views | 519 views |

Instagram recorded approximately 46.3 times as many raw views (13,060 / 282). Engaged views and viewers are different metrics and should not be compared as equivalent.

### YouTube traffic sources

Search 41.5%; channel pages 20.2%; Shorts feed 14.9%; other YouTube features 11.0%; external 9.2%; other sources 3.2%. The Shorts feed accounts for roughly 42 of 282 views.

Other reported YouTube metrics: 3.2 hours of watch time; 87.1% stayed to watch and 12.9% swiped away. The stayed-to-watch result comes from a small feed sample. It is not an overall retention or completion rate.

### Instagram reach and actions

99.7% of views came from non-followers; 100% came from Reels. The dashboard reported 206 interactions, 184 profile visits and 1 external link tap.

## 2. Why the gap

The strongest supported explanation is unequal feed distribution. Instagram is reaching people beyond the account's small follower base; YouTube is getting more traffic from search and channel pages than from the Shorts feed.

These data do not explain why YouTube's feed reach is low. They do not establish a shadowban, bad upload timing, watermark penalties or weak hooks. Different series and publication ages also prevent a same-clips comparison.

The leading Instagram Reel and leading YouTube Short are different items. Their 519-versus-14 comparison shows the observed reach gap, not a controlled test of the same content.

## 3. What's working

- Instagram discovery: non-follower reach is the clearest positive signal.
- YouTube search: search is finding viewers, but the actual search terms have not yet been pulled. Titles and descriptions may help discovery; attribution to wording is not proven.
- Early YouTube viewing signal: 87.1% stayed to watch is encouraging within the small sampled feed audience, not proof that every clip performs well.

## 4. What to do next

1. Measure matched clips: compare the same series and parts at the same age after posting. Record views, unique reach, feed share, watch behavior and audience growth. Keep each platform's metric definitions separate.
2. Keep the approved posting run consistent: retain the current 30-minute spacing and private-first claims checks. Treat continued posting as a way to collect evidence, not a guaranteed fix. No fixed timeline for YouTube feed pickup is established here.
3. Test the profile-to-link step: 1 tap across 184 profile visits is a rough 0.54% activity ratio, not a person-level conversion rate. Review the bio, link label and call to action, then change one element at a time and compare results. Any public changes remain subject to the existing approval rules.
4. Pull search terms next: use actual queries to inform future wording rather than guessing which phrases drove traffic.

The business bottleneck visible today is between attention and link activity. There is no affiliate-install, purchase or revenue evidence in this snapshot, so revenue impact remains unmeasured.
